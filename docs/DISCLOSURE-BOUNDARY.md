# Public disclosure boundary

**Status:** Foundation proposal  
**Applies to:** All public website content, public repository material, social posts and partner-facing summaries

## 1. Core rule

Share enough to make the mission understandable and the invitation credible. Do not publish information that exposes sensitive people, unvalidated operating methods, security controls, private relationships or unfinished product internals.

The website is a public introduction, not a data room.

## 2. Safe to communicate at principle level

Subject to factual review, public content may describe:
- the grassroots football opportunity problem being explored;
- the initiative's purpose and intended beneficiaries;
- high-level principles: discoverability, responsible verification, safeguarding, privacy, fairness and transparency;
- broad categories of people and organisations invited to collaborate;
- high-level project status, accurately labelled as research, design, prototype or operational only when evidenced;
- ways to make a general enquiry once an approved contact route exists.

## 3. Do not publish without explicit review and approval

- Player identities, profiles, contact details, locations, performance records or personal histories.
- Information about minors, guardians, consent records or safeguarding cases.
- Private partner, coach, academy, scout or stakeholder communications.
- Unpublished research, detailed domain models, database schemas, internal workflows or proprietary decision logic.
- Verification procedures, fraud-detection signals, risk thresholds or security-sensitive controls.
- Internal issue, incident, defect, threat or vulnerability details.
- Unvalidated statistics, unsupported market claims, implied endorsements or unconfirmed partnerships.
- Detailed implementation roadmaps that would expose unfinished mechanisms or create commitments.
- Credentials, tokens, private URLs, environment values or operational configuration.

## 4. Claims discipline

Every material public claim must have:
- a named owner;
- an identifiable source or evidence record;
- a date or review point;
- a status: verified, qualified, proposed, or prohibited;
- wording that does not exceed the evidence.

Never convert an ambition into a present-tense capability. Never imply endorsement, partnership, verification, reach, adoption or outcomes without permission and evidence.

## 5. Public status vocabulary

Use these labels consistently:
- **Exploring:** the problem or opportunity is being researched.
- **Designing:** a proposed approach is being specified.
- **Prototype:** a limited demonstration exists; it is not necessarily a live service.
- **Pilot:** a bounded, authorised test is underway with its scope stated.
- **Operational:** a service is live and supported by current evidence.

Do not use "verified", "approved", "partnered", "available" or "launched" casually.

## 6. Disclosure review checklist

Before publishing a page, image, statistic, announcement or downloadable asset, ask:

1. Is the statement true today?
2. Can we point to evidence and an accountable owner?
3. Does it reveal personal, safeguarding, security-sensitive or confidential information?
4. Could a reasonable visitor mistake a proposal for an operating service?
5. Does the wording promise access, selection, trials, contracts or placements?
6. Do we have the rights and permissions for every image, logo, quote and name?
7. Has the relevant reviewer approved publication?

If any answer is uncertain, hold publication and resolve the uncertainty first.

## 7. Open-source repository caution

The website repository is public. Treat every committed file, issue, pull request, asset, branch name and commit message as externally visible. Keep confidential working notes and sensitive evidence out of this repository. A later deletion does not guarantee that previously published material disappears from forks, caches or history.
